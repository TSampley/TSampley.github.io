Universities have gone through quite the transformation over their long history. Nearly always unique accumulations of knowledge, they range from private academies where primarily the privileged were prepared for the “burden” of their predecessors, to noble research institutes seeking to further human knowledge, to “higher” extensions of the labor factory that is the public education system. Trust but Verify

A degree is meant to be a type of certification that indicates a student has achieved some level of education and the college they attended has proved that. It’s a useful tool for employers trying to fill positions requiring specific knowledge or skills, but even with a degree, a typical interview will cover basic working knowledge to verify the candidate actually has the knowledge required for the position.

Ideally, the student cares personally about the knowledge, but the whole reason we need to certify that knowledge is because 1) people are often overconfident in their own knowledge and 2) people lie. I have trouble taking degrees seriously: I’ve overheard too many students collaborating during exams
