The world is becoming increasingly crowded. We're going to need to seriously talk about population control (it's already long overdue) - if-and-only-if we want to avoid excessive death and suffering. That might sound like a ridiculous caveat if you haven't been paying attention, but for better or worse, more and more capitalists are coming right out and saying the quiet part out-loud.
What's at Risk?
What if we don't control population? What happens if we continue with this status quo? The movie Idiocracy got it mostly right, but neglected that there are many wealthy people who are just as stupid and selfish as the rednecks portrayed - shout out to my former manager with 7 children who talked about his wife like she was only good for making babies 🤮. If we let population grow uncontrolled, we'll end up scrambling over resources, which will already skew towards those with privilege first and foremost, which, as we've all seen in modern society, ends up applying a positive selection pressure for sociopaths, narcissists, and good ol' US-individualists. The resultant death and suffering are incalculable and unknowable until the crush comes.
I come from a family with 3 children, and even that is too much for me ethically (though my brother and I came as a set when my mom really just wanted one more - oops). The simple (or naive) scenario entails a heterosexual couple having two children. Two people out, two people in. But at this point, we should be having only one child per couple.
If we, as self-aware, intelligent human beings, can recognize that is the inevitable end of a freely breeding population like any other animal, then we have a clear obligation to circumvent it.
The Options

With other organisms, we're willing to knockout genes in embryos and see what changes emerge to determine their effect. This has obvious issues when applied to humans.
Our current understanding of most genetics is based on taking samples from loads of different people, paired with as much phenotype information as possible, then cross-referencing the two to find genetic similarities between individuals with matching phenotypes and dissimilarities between individuals without.
