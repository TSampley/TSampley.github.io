A revolution is any political change that occurs outside of a system to change that system. All forms of political systems allow change in one way or another – if not by dictated by the single, holy ruler alone – but who is allowed to change that system and how is limited to widely varying degrees. Sometimes a system may allow change but not fast or dramatically enough to appease some population's concerns, and when the cost of not changing greatly exceeds the cost of enacting change outside the system, that is when the people will pursue revolution.

## Fascist Revolution
A fascist revolution is carried out by a small group with minority support of the population.

## Puppet Revolution
A puppet revolution is supported externally by another nation, usually more powerful, though not always (see Israel influence over USA) – a form of new colonialism[^1].

## Popular Revolution
Most revolutions through history are popular revolutions. 

## Democratic Revolution
Because democracy requires the consent of the governed, a democratic revolution can only happen in public.

Contrast with a fascist revolution, which, by its exclusionary nature, easily operates in secret societies in private and dog whistles in public.

A democratic revolution is probably the most difficult to carry out successfully, because most – if not all – efforts and coordination is done in public, easily scrutinized and potentially disrupted by enemies.
