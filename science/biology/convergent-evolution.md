Convergent evolution is the phenomenon where multiple, separate lineages develop adaptations that serve the same function, usually with the same form. The popular, elementary example is the development of fins in both [bony-fish, like salmon and lungfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osteichthyes), and [cetaceans, like dolphins and whales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cetacean).

Since adaptations are judged by their environment, depending on their strength in the environment, rewarded by proliferating or punished by withering to nothing, when an adaptation is found in many species, we can infer that the adaptation is consistently strong within that environment.
