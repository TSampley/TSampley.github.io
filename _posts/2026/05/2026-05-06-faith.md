We should never let sentiment dictate our actions; however, it's the first place we start any kind of thought process. The important step is to take that initial feeling and turn our attention back on it to assess it for alignment with reality.

## Sentiment

All **faiths** must be **exterminated** for **humanity** to make **progress**.

**faiths** - Belief systems that take an assumption/conclusion regardless of any evidence or reason/logic.
**extermination** - Removed from existence.
**humanity** - The collective of those considered "human".
**progress** - Deepening our understanding of the world and reality.

### Assess for Precision

There is some progress being made every day; however, much of that progress is being erroded by faith-based interests, resulting in a net 0 or negative effect on knowledge. The less influence faith has, the more progress can be easily made and maintained.

Why can't we just "leave them alone"? Because they exist. They live and act in the world, realizing their deficiencies. That is harm. Why would you allow something to exist that you know is causing harm? That would make you cruel and qualify you as evil yourself. Either you understand that the problem needs to be fixed, or you are the problem that needs to be fixed.

So while the original statement isn't as black-and-white as it is originally presented, the overall effect is the same: an ethical human being works to remove faith from existence.

### Faith

But what if we assess something for precision while assuming that the conclusion must be true regardless of any evidence? There's hardly any point in assessing the precision of our statements. The whole reason we try to assess the precision of our statements is to ensure our conclusion is valid and follows from those statements. If we assume from the beginning that our conclusion must be true, we lose any reason to worry about precision. We lose any reason to think critically about our beliefs or the statements of anyone else at all.

The Hard Problem of Faith is that – while you can *reason* with the faithful – you cannot use logic to change their conclusion. If someone truly has faith – if someone has taken that first major leap of assuming their conclusion is true and cannot possibly be false – then any effort spent trying to reason with them is bound to fail from the beginning. Fortunately, faith is largely performative and people often lose "faith" after some significant event that forces them to grapple with newly realized implications of their willful ignorance. Unfortunately, these events are difficult to contrive for some given person, on the spot, so the loss of faith is largely up to luck, which means the only way that we can remove faith intentionally is by force.
